## Broad Ripple (Indianapolis neighborhood, not a separate municipality)

- What it is: a neighborhood/cultural district on Indianapolis's north side, in Washington Township, along the White River — historically its own incorporated town
- Boundaries: roughly the White River, Evanston Ave., Kessler Blvd., and Meridian St. (Encyclopedia of Indianapolis)
- Distance from downtown Indianapolis: ~6 mi north (with a slight easterly component)
- History: platted April 1837 (two adjoining plats merged 1884); incorporated as its own town 1894; annexed into Indianapolis 1922 (after failed annexation attempts in 1906, 1909, 1913)
- Landmarks: Butler University nearby; the Monon Trail runs through; Broad Ripple Avenue's commercial/cultural strip (restaurants, galleries, the Indianapolis Art Center); Broad Ripple Park (62 acres, on the White River); the historic Central Canal
- Approx. center: 39.8667, -86.1417
- **Population/size caveat:** Wikipedia's infobox cites 7,461 (2020) across 10.455 sq mi for "Broad Ripple Village," but that boundary looks broader than the walkable commercial core most people mean by "Broad Ripple" — don't present it as a precise, authoritative neighborhood figure without checking what boundary the city itself uses.

> Broad Ripple is a neighborhood and designated cultural district on the north side of Indianapolis, about six miles north of downtown along the White River, where the Monon Trail runs through and Butler University sits nearby. The area was first platted in 1837, incorporated as its own town in 1894, and annexed into Indianapolis in 1922. Its commercial core along Broad Ripple Avenue — restaurants, galleries, the Indianapolis Art Center — plus Broad Ripple Park and the historic Central Canal give it a distinct identity within the city.

## The 9 Marion County civil townships

Not incorporated towns — civil townships within Marion County / Indianapolis
(Unigov). All nine were laid out the same day, **April 16, 1822**, several
initially paired administratively (insufficient population to stand alone)
and split later — noted per township. Population is the Census's
township-level county-subdivision figure (includes any cities/towns inside
it, e.g. Speedway, Beech Grove).

**Locator-map layout** (confirmed via each township's own stated borders —
a clean 3×3 grid, Center Township containing downtown is the middle tile):

```
        Pike        Washington     Lawrence
        Wayne         Center        Warren
       Decatur         Perry       Franklin
```
Decatur touches Perry via a water boundary (the White River) rather than a
straight line; Decatur also touches Center at a small corner along with
Wayne.

### Lawrence Township
- NE corner of Marion County. Pop.: 118,447 (2010) → 122,321 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Washington until split Sept 4, 1826). ~46.9 sq mi.
- Borders: Hamilton Co. (N), Hancock Co. (E), Warren Twp. (S), Washington Twp. (W). Center: 39.8800, -86.0103.
- Landmarks: Oaklandon Historic District (settled 1849, on the old Indianapolis & Bellefontaine Railroad); former Fort Benjamin Harrison, now Fort Harrison State Park.

> Lawrence Township sits in the northeast corner of Marion County, bordered by Hamilton County to the north, Hancock County to the east, Warren Township to the south, and Washington Township to the west. Its population grew from 118,447 in 2010 to 122,321 in the 2020 census. The township was organized on April 16, 1822, and today includes the Oaklandon Historic District and the former Fort Benjamin Harrison, now a state park.

### Warren Township
- East side, due east of Center Twp. Pop.: 99,433 (2010) → 106,191 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 as combined "Centre-Warren"; split May 1, 1826. ~48.3 sq mi.
- Borders: Lawrence Twp. (N), Franklin Twp. (S), Center Twp. (W), Hancock Co. (E). Center: 39.7750, -86.0172.
- Landmarks: named for Joseph Warren; includes part of Beech Grove, west half of Cumberland, Warren Park neighborhood.

> Warren Township sits on the east side of Marion County, bordered by Lawrence Township to the north, Franklin Township to the south, Center Township to the west, and Hancock County to the east. Population grew from 99,433 in 2010 to 106,191 in the 2020 census. It was organized on April 16, 1822 — initially combined with Center Township as "Centre-Warren" until the two split in 1826.

### Wayne Township
- West side, due west of Center Twp. Pop.: 136,828 (2010) → 148,444 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Pike until split May 10, 1824). ~48.9 sq mi.
- Borders: Pike Twp. (N), Center Twp. (E), Decatur Twp. (S), Hendricks Co. (W). Center: 39.7711, -86.2614.
- Landmarks: north half of Indianapolis International Airport; the town of Speedway sits within/adjacent to it; named for Anthony Wayne.

> Wayne Township sits on the west side of Marion County, bordered by Pike Township to the north, Center Township to the east, Decatur Township to the south, and Hendricks County to the west. Population grew from 136,828 in 2010 to 148,444 in the 2020 census. It was organized on April 16, 1822 and includes the north half of Indianapolis International Airport and the town of Speedway.

### Perry Township
- South side. Pop.: 108,972 (2010) → 121,768 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Decatur/Franklin; separated 1823–1824). ~45.8 sq mi.
- Borders: Center Twp. (N), Franklin Twp. (E), Decatur Twp. (W, across the White River), Johnson Co. (S). Center: 39.6817, -86.1344.
- Landmarks: named for Oliver Hazard Perry; home to Southport (platted 1849) and the University of Indianapolis (founded 1902).
- *Fair Housing note: source material for Perry Township included detailed demographic/racial-composition content, deliberately excluded here.*

> Perry Township sits on the south side of Marion County, bordered by Center Township to the north, Franklin Township to the east, Decatur Township to the west across the White River, and Johnson County to the south. Population grew from 108,972 in 2010 to 121,768 in the 2020 census. It was organized on April 16, 1822 and is home to Southport, platted in 1849, and the University of Indianapolis, founded in 1902.

### Franklin Township
- SE corner. Pop.: 32,080 (2000) → 54,594 (2010) → 65,788 (2020) — fastest-growing of the nine, 2000–2020.
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Decatur/Perry; separate by May 12, 1824). ~42.1 sq mi.
- Borders: Warren & Perry Twps. (N/W), Shelby & Hancock Co. (E), Johnson Co. (S). Center: 39.6803, -86.0275.
- Landmarks: named for Benjamin Franklin; Marion County Fairgrounds; Beech Grove Railroad (Amtrak) shops on its Perry Twp. boundary.

> Franklin Township sits in the southeast corner of Marion County, bordered by Warren and Perry townships to the north and west, Shelby and Hancock counties to the east, and Johnson County to the south. Population roughly doubled between 2000 and 2020 — from 32,080 to 65,788, passing through 54,594 in 2010 — the fastest growth of Marion County's nine townships over that span. It was organized on April 16, 1822.

### Pike Township
- NW corner. Pop.: 77,895 (2010) → 83,030 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Wayne until split May 10, 1824). ~41.5 sq mi.
- Borders: Hamilton & Boone Co. (N), Washington Twp. (E), Wayne Twp. (S), Hendricks Co. (W). Center: 39.8711, -86.2531.
- Landmarks: Eagle Creek Park (~5,000 acres, 1,400-acre reservoir); Lafayette Square Mall (1968); Park 100 industrial complex (1974); I-65/I-465 north junction. Named for Zebulon Pike.

> Pike Township sits in the northwest corner of Marion County, bordered by Hamilton and Boone counties to the north, Washington Township to the east, Wayne Township to the south, and Hendricks County to the west. Population grew from 77,895 in 2010 to 83,030 in the 2020 census. It was organized on April 16, 1822 and today contains Eagle Creek Park, Lafayette Square Mall, and the north junction of I-65 and I-465.

### Washington Township
- North-central, due north of Center Twp. Pop.: 132,049 (2010) → 138,678 (2020).
- Organized Apr 16, 1822 (paired with Lawrence until split Sept 4, 1826). ~48.5 sq mi.
- Borders: Hamilton Co. (N), Lawrence Twp. (E), Center Twp. (S), Pike Twp. (W). Center: 39.8697, -86.1442.
- Landmarks: contains Broad Ripple Village (see above) and Castleton Square, Indiana's largest mall; Butler University.

> Washington Township sits in north-central Marion County, bordered by Hamilton County to the north, Lawrence Township to the east, Center Township to the south, and Pike Township to the west. Population grew from 132,049 in 2010 to 138,678 in the 2020 census. It was organized on April 16, 1822 — initially combined with Lawrence Township until the two split in 1826 — and today includes Broad Ripple Village and Castleton Square, Indiana's largest mall.

### Decatur Township
- SW corner. Pop.: 24,726 (2000) → 32,388 (2010) → 36,951 (2020) — 2nd-fastest-growing of the nine, 2000–2020.
- Organized Apr 16, 1822. ~32.3 sq mi.
- Borders: Wayne & Center Twps. (N), Perry Twp. (E, across the White River), Morgan Co. (S), Hendricks Co. (W). Center: 39.6956, -86.2617.
- Landmarks: Indianapolis International Airport main terminal; AmeriPlex industrial park (~1,500 acres). Named for Stephen Decatur.

> Decatur Township sits in the southwest corner of Marion County, bordered by Wayne and Center townships to the north, Perry Township to the east across the White River, Morgan County to the south, and Hendricks County to the west. Population grew from 32,388 in 2010 to 36,951 in the 2020 census — the second-fastest growth of Marion County's nine townships between 2000 and 2020. It was organized on April 16, 1822, and its landmarks include the Indianapolis International Airport terminal and the AmeriPlex industrial park.

### Center Township
- Contains downtown Indianapolis itself. Pop.: 142,787 (2010) → 153,549 (2020) — down from a historical peak of 337,211 in 1950.
- Organized spring 1822 (paired with Warren as "Centre-Warren" until split May 1, 1826). 42.7 sq mi (41.85 land / 0.84 water).
- Borders: Washington Twp. (N), Warren Twp. (E), Perry Twp. (S), Wayne Twp. (W); touches Decatur at a small SW corner. Center: 39.7736, -86.1394.
- Landmarks: White River State Park, Crown Hill Cemetery, the Indianapolis Downtown Heliport.

> Center Township sits at the center of Marion County and contains downtown Indianapolis, bordered by Washington Township to the north, Warren Township to the east, Perry Township to the south, and Wayne Township to the west. Population was 142,787 in 2010 and 153,549 in the 2020 census, down from a peak of 337,211 in 1950. It was organized in the spring of 1822 — initially combined with Warren Township as "Centre-Warren" until the two split in 1826 — and today includes White River State Park and the Indianapolis Downtown Heliport.
